METHODOLOGY

Designed to test a theory, not assume it.

FlockWatch collects public coverage of Flock Safety, labels the channel, preserves the original link, detects explicit sponsorship and press-release distribution, and groups stories that share long runs of identical text.

How stories get grouped

Two stories are linked only when they share contiguous sequences of eight or more identical words, or five where only a short feed snippet was captured. Independent reporters covering the same incident share vocabulary; they do not share long word runs. The overlapping passage itself is stored and displayed so the grouping can be checked rather than trusted.

An earlier version grouped stories by which watch phrase they contained, so every story using “within minutes” was presented as one amplification cluster. That produced confident-looking findings from coincidence, and it has been removed.

Why critical coverage is collected too

The monitor deliberately searches for lawsuits, audits, privacy objections, cancelled contracts and studies finding no effect, and keeps them in the same dataset. Without that denominator an outlet running twelve promotional stories looks identical whether or not it also ran five accountability pieces, and the tool could only ever confirm the theory it was built to test.

What this cannot tell you

  • Who wrote a passage first. Capture order reflects when the monitor saw an item and what date its feed reported, not provenance.
  • Whether money changed hands. Only explicit sponsorship labels are evidence of payment; nothing else here is.
  • Whether a police post was prompted by Flock. That question is answered by the responsive communications, which is what the records builder is for.
  • The full picture of any outlet. Only Flock-related coverage is collected, so these counts describe one beat, not a newsroom.

What the labels mean

Agency-originated
Published by a police department, sheriff, city, or other public body.
Editorial
Published by a newsroom without an observed sponsorship disclosure.
Sponsored
The publisher explicitly labels the item as sponsored, presented by, or paid for.
Press release
Company-authored material distributed through a press-release wire or copied from one.
Unknown
The public page does not establish who initiated or paid for the item.
Promotional / Critical
Detected stance of the item, from language about outcomes and credit on one side and privacy, litigation, audits, misuse or cancellation on the other. Keyword-based and approximate; correct it at the source before relying on a count.
Syndicated copy
Near-identical text under more than one publisher name, typically a station group or wire pickup. Count it as one story.

A reuse lane is an investigative lead. It does not by itself establish payment, hidden sponsorship, or coordination.
